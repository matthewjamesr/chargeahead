The academic questions

This project is equal parts infrastructure planning and applied machine-learning methodology — in particular, how honestly a geospatial model can be evaluated.

RQ1 · Demand

What predicts fast-charging demand?

Which observable features of a place — its people, jobs, vehicles, roads, and existing supply — actually explain where DC fast charging exists and thrives? Demographics alone, it turns out, explain almost nothing.

RQ2 · Validation

How do you validate a map?

Random train/test splits leak badly on geographic data — a site and its near-twin across the street land on both sides. We use county-grouped spatial cross-validation throughout, and report the unflattering numbers it produces.

RQ3 · Gaps

Where is the underserved demand?

Ranking tracts with high predicted demand and zero fast chargers produces a defensible shortlist for planners — with the model's own confidence limits attached, not hidden.

Results — model v2, DC fast charging

Target: DC fast-charge ports per census tract. Algorithm: gradient-boosted trees (scikit-learn HistGradientBoostingRegressor). Validation: 5-fold cross-validation grouped by county, so the model is always scored on counties it has never seen.

Methods, in the open

  1. Census the present. Pull every public, available U.S. charging station from the NLR Alternative Fuel Stations API (83,546 stations) via scripts/fetch_nlr_stations.py.
  2. Geocode to tracts. Spatially join stations to 2023 census tracts; aggregate Level 2 and DC fast ports per tract (83,528 of 83,546 matched).
  3. Build features. Join ACS demographics, distribute ZIP-level data (EV registrations, business patterns) to tracts with population-density-weighted areal interpolation, measure highway distance and out-of-tract supply context.
  4. Train & honestly validate. Gradient-boosted trees with county-grouped cross-validation; report R²/MAE on unseen counties only.
  5. Rank the gaps. Score every tract; the candidate list is zero-DCF tracts ordered by predicted ports. Full code: models/build_model_v2.py.

Demographics — population, population density, median household income (ACS 2023 5-year, tract level).

EV registrations — plug-in vehicles by ZIP (WA Dept. of Licensing; CA DMV), allocated to tracts. CA + WA only for now.

Employment — jobs and establishments by ZIP (Census ZIP Business Patterns 2022), allocated to tracts.

Corridor access — distance from tract centroid to the nearest TIGER primary road (Interstates & U.S. highways).

Supply context — DC fast ports within 5 km and 10 km of the tract centroid (excluding the tract itself), and distance to the nearest out-of-tract DCFC station.

Target — DC fast-charge ports physically located in the tract (NLR census, Oct 2026).

Geospatial models fail quietly when validated naively: nearby tracts are near-duplicates, so random splits let the model "memorize the neighborhood" and report fantasy scores. Every ChargeAhead model is therefore validated with GroupKFold cross-validation grouped by county — all tracts of a county sit entirely in train or entirely in test. Feature importance is permutation importance measured on a held-out sample, not training data. When a number on this page changes between versions, the change was earned under the same protocol.

  • Utilization is unobserved. No public dataset reports sessions/day per station; the model predicts where ports are, a proxy for where demand is — placement history and network strategy are baked into the target.
  • Supply features cut both ways. "Ports within 5 km" is the strongest predictor, so v2 is best read as an infill model. Greenfield discovery is a separate family, in development.
  • EV registration coverage is two states. CA/WA publish granular registration data; most states do not. Features are missing — honestly, as NaN — elsewhere.
  • ZIP→tract allocation is approximate. Areal interpolation assumes demand follows population density within a ZIP.
  • A ranking is not a site. Grid capacity, land cost, permitting and equity constraints decide real placements; the model produces a shortlist for humans.

One project, many model families

ChargeAhead is a home for a family of placement models, each framed as its own research question with its own validation. New families land here as they mature.

Tract Demand — GBDT

Live · v2

The flagship on this page: gradient-boosted trees predicting DC fast-charge ports for all 85,186 U.S. census tracts. Spatial-CV R² 0.209.

Greenfield Discovery

In development

The same target with supply-context features removed, isolating genuinely underserved demand from network clustering — the honest "where is nobody looking?" model.

Corridor Model

Planned

Highway-segment framing for corridor fast charging (NEVI-style): traffic volumes, stop spacing, and gap analysis along Interstates rather than residential tracts.

Utilization Model

Blocked on data

Predicting sessions per day — the target that actually matters commercially — the moment a network or city publishes station-level utilization openly. The pipeline is ready for it.
